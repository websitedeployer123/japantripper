The Trip
in Plain English

A 45-day Japan workation from Tuesday Sep 15 to Thursday Oct 29, 2026. The plan mixes anime/Nujabes pilgrimages, major food days, autumn nature, a few high-ticket experiences, and enough work blocks to keep the trip realistic.

Start Big in TokyoArrive, recover, explore Shibuya/Akihabara/Odaiba, do the helicopter flight, eat omakase, and take the GT-R/RX-7 drive into Hakone.
Go North for Wild JapanNikko, Ginzan Onsen, Sendai, Matsushima, Naruko Gorge and the snow monkeys give the trip its mountain/onsen spine.
Then Culture + FoodKyoto, Nara, Osaka and Kōya-san carry the temples, sword class, street food, kaiseki, JJK/Naruto energy and night photography.
Westward PilgrimageHiroshima and Miyajima slow the pace down, then Shikoku adds Dōgo Onsen and Naruto whirlpools before returning through Ise.
Finish in HokkaidoSapporo, Daisetsuzan, Asahikawa, Lake Tōya, Noboribetsu and Otaru close the trip with seafood, volcanoes and autumn landscapes.
Optional Race PivotIf we get Tokyo Legacy Half Marathon 2026 entry, keep Sunday Oct 18 flexible and reroute the Shikoku/Ise stretch around Tokyo.

Nikkō, Tochigi
Japan's Most Ornate Shrine + Waterfall
Tōshō-gū Shrine — layered gold, carved sleeping cat, three wise monkeys, ancient cedar forest. Mid-September the maples just begin to warm. Walk up to Kegon Falls (97m drop) through Chūzenji forest. Onsen at Nikko Kanaya Hotel — Japan's oldest mountain resort. Stay overnight or return to Tokyo for next day.

Day12–13Tohoku
Ginzan Onsen · Yamadera
Spirited Away Onsen Town + Cliff Temple
Day 12: Yamadera (Risshaku-ji) — 1,000-year-old temple complex carved into a cliff, 1,015 stone steps to summit. The view over the valley is staggering. Day 13: Ginzan Onsen — a Taishō-era (1912) wooden ryokan village on a river gorge. Said to have visually inspired Spirited Away's spirit bathhouse town. Lanterns after dark, snow-ready wooden balconies, powerful rotenburo. Stay one night here — it's essential.

Jujutsu Kaisen Pilgrimage Day 1

Nishiki Market ("Kyoto's kitchen") for street snacks: octopus skewers, pickles, tofu doughnuts.

Its stone lanterns, ancient torii, and stone steps through cryptomeria trees are unmistakable. Midday: Shinkansen or rapid train to Osaka (15 min). Check in near Dotonbori. Day30Kōya-san
Kōya-san, Wakayama
Sacred Mountain: Buddhism's Most Powerful Place
Day trip or overnight from Osaka (1h45m by Nankai Rail). Ōkunoin Cemetery — 200,000 moss-covered stone lanterns and tombstones of samurai lords in ancient cedar forest. The most spiritually powerful place in Japan. Walk the 2km cemetery path at dusk when lanterns glow. Kongōbu-ji Temple — the head temple of Shingon Buddhism with Japan's largest rock garden. Stay overnight in a shukubō (temple lodging) and attend 6am morning prayer service.

Day31Hiroshima
Hiroshima · Peace Memorial
The Most Important Day of the Trip
Peace Memorial Park and Museum — allow 3–4 hours, unhurried. The A-Bomb Dome ruin, the Children's Peace Monument, the museum's personal objects of those lost. A singular experience that reframes everything. Afternoon: walk Hiroshima's excellent food scene — Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki with noodles inside (completely different from Osaka style) at Micchan or Hassho. Shukkei-en Garden for late-autumn evening walk.

Day32Miyajima
Miyajima Island
The Floating Torii Gate at Dawn
Ferry from Hiroshima to Itsukushima (Miyajima). Stay overnight on the island — when day-trippers leave, you have the floating vermilion O-torii gate almost to yourself at sunrise. Walk to it at low tide on the mudflat. Itsukushima Shrine (UNESCO) with autumn leaves. Hike Mt. Misen (535m) through primeval forest to the highest viewpoint in the Seto Inland Sea. Deer wander the island freely. Try grilled momiji-manju (maple-leaf shaped cakes).

Day34Naruto
Naruto City, Tokushima
Naruto Whirlpools — The City That Shares the Name
Naruto, Tokushima — the actual city that inspired the whirlpool symbolism in Naruto (and the "Naruto Uzumaki" spirals are literally based on the naruto fish-cake swirl pattern). Take a sightseeing boat under the Ōnaruto Bridge to watch the giant tidal whirlpools — up to 20m across during spring tides. The Onaruto Bridge glass walkway over the whirlpools is spectacular. 2026 calendar note: this lands on Sunday Oct 18, the same date as Tokyo Legacy Half Marathon 2026. Day35–36Ise
Ise, Mie Prefecture
Ise Grand Shrine — Japan's Most Sacred Place
Ise Jingū — holiest Shinto site in Japan, dedicated to Amaterasu, the sun goddess. Rebuilt every 20 years in the exact same ancient cypress-wood style — the deepest forest shrine in the country. Walk Oharaimachi Street's Edo-period merchant street for akafuku mochi (red bean rice cakes made since 1707). Day 36: Ago Bay and Ise-Shima National Park — pearl cultivation bays, dramatic coastline, fresh abalone and lobster at ama diver seafood restaurants. Kayak the inlet.

Sapporo, Hokkaido
Hokkaido Capital — Ramen Alley & Autumn Parks
Fly from Tokyo to Sapporo New Chitose (1.5hr). Susukino Ramen Alley (Rāmen Yokochō) — the original, the real deal. Sapporo miso ramen with corn and butter in narrow lantern-lit alley. Day41–42Nature
Daisetsuzan · Asahikawa
Japan's Wildest National Park — Peak Autumn
Daisetsuzan National Park — Japan's largest national park, 2,290m volcanic peaks, alpine lakes, bear habitat, and the most dramatic autumn foliage colour in the country (peak: mid-to-late October). Take the Asahidake Ropeway into the high volcanic plateau. Hike Asahidake peak for volcanic steam vents and 360° coloured mountain views. Kurodake trail for deep forest paths. Stay at Asahidake Onsen — soaking in outdoor baths while forest steams around you is transcendent.

Day43Nature
Lake Tōya · Noboribetsu
Volcanic Caldera Lake + Hell Valley Onsen
Lake Tōya — a perfectly circular volcanic caldera lake with an island in the centre. Crystal clear blue water, Mt. Yōtei (Hokkaido's Fuji) visible from the shore. Afternoon: Noboribetsu — Japan's most impressive onsen resort with Jigokudani ("Hell Valley"), a volcanic crater oozing sulphurous steam. Multiple different mineral baths in one ryokan — the variety of water types is found nowhere else. Stay overnight.

Work from one of the canal-side cafés. Lunch: fresh uni donburi. Afternoon: Otaru Music Box Museum, Kitaichi Glass (handmade Hokkaido glassware). Evening: back to Sapporo for final night dinner.

Experiences

The non-negotiables. Book these 2–3 months in advance — some sell out entirely or require concierge arrangements.

🚗
Day 8 · Hakone
Rent a Nissan GT-R R35 or Mazda RX-7 FD
Drive Japan's most legendary JDM cars on mountain roads designed for them. Hakone Turnpike: 10.3km of pure switchbacks, no speed traps on the toll section. Mt. Fuji visible from the summit on clear September mornings. Operators: J-Dream, Prestige Motor Rent, Liberty Walk car rental. Book 2–3 months early. Full-day rental ¥80,000–200,000 depending on model. Also consider the Izu Skyline route (35km ridge road, more remote).
📍 Tokyo pickup → Hakone Turnpike → Izu Skyline
🚁
Day 4 · Odaiba
Helicopter Tour over Tokyo at Sunset
Book the sunset slot from Odaiba Heliport with Nakanihon Air or Noevir Helicopters. 20–30 minute flight over Shinjuku skyline, Rainbow Bridge, Tokyo Bay. On clear September evenings, Mt. Fuji appears on the western horizon. Cost: ¥35,000–60,000/person. The view of Tokyo's scale from above — 14 million people, endless neon, highway loops — is humbling. Book 1–2 months ahead.
📍 Odaiba Heliport, Minato Ward
🏔️
Days 41–42 · Hokkaido
Daisetsuzan Peak Autumn Hike
Japan's largest and wildest national park. Take the Asahidake Ropeway to 1,600m, then hike the volcanic plateau as Japan's earliest autumn colour blazes below (mid-October, peak). Steam vents, bear warning signs, empty trails. Stay at Asahidake Onsen at the foot — outdoor baths, forest silence, zero crowds. The most dramatic natural experience in Japan in autumn.
📍 Higashikawa, Hokkaido
⛩️
Day 20 · Kyoto
Fushimi Inari at 5am
The 10,000 vermilion torii gates at dawn with zero crowds — maybe 3–4 people on the mountain path. Walk the full 4km loop to the summit. The gates narrow, the light filters orange-red, foxes occasionally appear on the path. JJK fans: this is the closest real-world equivalent to domain expansion visual design. Bring a headlamp for the first 30 minutes.
📍 Fushimi Ward, Kyoto
🥢
Day 7 · Tokyo
Omakase Sushi: Full Michelin Experience
Book Sushi Yoshitake (3-star, intimate 7-seat counter), Harutaka, or Sushi Sho — Tokyo's legendary counter sushi in a city where sushi is a performing art. 15–20 courses, chef selects everything. Book via Tableall or Omakase apps 2–3 months ahead. Budget ¥40,000–70,000. The absolute pinnacle of Japanese cuisine in 90 minutes.
📍 Ginza or Akasaka, Tokyo

Anime Pilgrimages
& Locations

Real places that inspired, directly appeared in, or are spiritually connected to Naruto and Jujutsu Kaisen — plus where to find the best merch.

🍥 Naruto · Shippuden
Naruto City, Tokushima
📍 Naruto, Tokushima, Shikoku
The city that gave the anime its name — and the tidal Naruto Whirlpools that inspired Naruto Uzumaki's spiral seal and surname. The word "naruto" literally means whirlpool. 🍥 Naruto — Forest Training Arcs
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
📍 Arashiyama, Kyoto
The towering, claustrophobic bamboo path that Naruto and his team run through in countless training and mission arcs has a clear visual ancestor here. 🍥 Naruto — Hidden Leaf Village
Nikkō Shrine Complex
📍 Nikkō, Tochigi
The ornate wooden architecture, massive cedar forests, and winding stone stairways of Nikkō's Tōshō-gū complex are central aesthetic references for the Hidden Leaf Village (Konohagakure). The carved gates, forest atmosphere, and the sense of a hidden settlement protected by ancient trees are all here in full effect.
💜 Jujutsu Kaisen
Fushimi Inari Taisha
📍 Fushimi Ward, Kyoto
The 10,000 torii gates of Fushimi Inari are the single most JJK-adjacent location in Japan. The visual language of JJK — compressed red gates, winding dark paths, fox spirits (kitsune are cursed-spirit adjacent) — is drawn directly from Inari worship sites. The upper mountain at night, lit by stone lanterns, feels like entering a Domain Expansion.
💜 Jujutsu Kaisen — Kyoto Arc
Yasaka Shrine, Gion
📍 Gion, Kyoto
The Kyoto Jujutsu Tech school storyline is visually and spiritually rooted in Gion's ancient shrine district. Yasaka Shrine's stone lanterns, enormous torii, and cryptomeria forest exactly match the backgrounds in the Kyoto Goodwill Event arc. Walk Hanamikōji at dusk and the feeling of cursed spirits in old Kyoto is remarkably convincing.
💜 Jujutsu Kaisen
Tōkyō — Shibuya Incident Arc
📍 Shibuya, Tokyo
The Shibuya Incident Arc is set entirely in Shibuya's real geography. Stand at Shibuya Crossing and visualize the barrier dome. The Underground Concourse, Scramble Square, and the Mark City overpass are all explicitly depicted in the manga/anime. The Metro Ticket Gate where Geto seals the non-sorcerers is based on the real Shibuya Station underground layout.
💜 Jujutsu Kaisen — Opening Scene
Waseda University Area
📍 Shinjuku, Tokyo
The Tokyo Jujutsu High School is visually based on buildings near Waseda University's Waseda campus. The stone walls, campus paths, and old academic architecture in the Takadanobaba / Waseda area match exactly. Walk the campus and the surrounding traditional student neighbourhood for that JJK school-arc atmosphere.
🎌 Anime General — Spirited Away
Ginzan Onsen + Dōgo Onsen
📍 Yamagata / Matsuyama
Two confirmed visual inspirations for the Spirited Away bathhouse: Ginzan Onsen (Taishō-era wooden ryokan on a river, lantern-lit at night — the most direct design reference) and Dōgo Onsen Honkan in Matsuyama (3,000-year-old bathhouse that Miyazaki directly cited). Autumn · Forests · Mountains · Coasts

Nature & Scenic
Highlights

Sep–Oct is Japan's best season for nature. Autumn begins in Hokkaido in mid-September and sweeps south reaching Kyoto/Tokyo by late October. These are the crown jewels.

🍁 Peak: Mid–Late October
Daisetsuzan National Park
📍 Hokkaido
Japan's largest national park and wildest wilderness. Volcanic peaks over 2,000m, alpine tundra, Japan's earliest and most dramatic autumn colour. Bear, deer, fox. Asahidake Ropeway into the volcanic plateau. Completely uncrowded by international standards.
⏱ 2 full days minimum. Stay at Asahidake Onsen.
🌊 September Oyster Peak
Matsushima Bay Islands
📍 Miyagi, Tohoku
260 pine-covered islands in a tranquil bay — one of Japan's three officially great views. Boat tour through the islands at sunrise. Fresh September oysters on the pier. The most serene bayscapes in Honshu.
⏱ Half-day or overnight from Sendai.
🍁 Peak: Early October
Naruko Onsen Gorge
📍 Osaki, Miyagi
A 100m-deep canyon with sheer walls turning fiery crimson in early October. Almost no international tourists. Traditional kokeshi doll craftsmen. Small steaming onsen town at the rim of the gorge. Japan's best-kept autumn secret.
⏱ Full day. Combine with Sendai.
🌋 Year-round
Jigokudani Snow Monkey Park
📍 Yamanouchi, Nagano
Wild Japanese macaques bathing in natural volcanic hot springs. October brings cool air and morning steam — the monkeys are more active, the autumn leaves frame the pools. Arrive by 7am to miss coach tour groups. 30-minute forest hike to reach the springs.
⏱ Half day. Combine with Karuizawa.
🍁 Late October
Kōya-san Ancient Cedar Forest
📍 Wakayama
The Ōkunoin approach — 2km of enormous cedar trees, mossy stone lanterns and samurai tombs. In late October the high-altitude forest turns gold and the stone lanterns create a tunnel of amber light. Stay overnight in a temple for the absolute full atmosphere.
⏱ Overnight stay essential.
🌊 September / October
Ise-Shima National Park
📍 Mie Prefecture
Rocky peninsula, pearl farm bays, hidden coves. Ama women divers still free-dive for abalone in traditional white garments. Kayak the Ago Bay inlets. Fresh lobster and abalone at ama huts on the coast — some of Japan's most unique dining experiences.
⏱ 1–2 days from Ise City.
🌋 Year-round
Lake Tōya Volcanic Caldera
📍 Hokkaido
A perfectly circular lake formed in a volcanic crater 110,000 years ago, 180m deep, with an island rising from the centre. Mt. Yōtei (Hokkaido's cone-shaped Fuji) visible across the lake. Late October: shore trees are full gold and red. Exceptionally clear water — you can see the lake floor in the shallows.
⏱ Half day. Combine with Noboribetsu.
🏝 September
Miyajima Island: Mt. Misen Forest
📍 Hiroshima Prefecture
Beyond the famous torii gate, Miyajima's Mt. Misen is covered in primeval forest that's been sacred since the 8th century. The summit (535m) gives views over the entire Seto Inland Sea. Ancient "sacred fire" burning since Kūkai lit it 1,200 years ago. Wild deer. No development. One of Japan's most quietly spiritual hikes.
⏱ Full day. Stay overnight on island.
🌲 September / October
Shiraito Falls, Karuizawa
📍 Nagano Prefecture
A curtain of 80 separate white streams cascading over a 70m basalt ledge — "white thread falls." Surrounded by forest that's just beginning to turn gold in late September. Famous enough to appear on old currency but still relatively easy to visit. Combine with the elegant Karuizawa resort town.
⏱ Half day from Karuizawa Station.

Trail

The club where Nujabes' collaborators, friends and label artists hold tribute events — most notably around the anniversary of his death each February. Underground hip-hop and lo-fi nights run year-round. First floor is a bar lounge; the basement is the main dancefloor with a surprisingly high ceiling for an underground club. Day 10 · Afternoon · Shibuya
Face Records — Recommended by Nujabes' Brother
Nujabes owned two Shibuya record shops — Tribe and Guinness Records — both closed after his death in 2010. The closest living equivalent: Face Records Shibuya, personally recommended by Nujabes' brother as the best place to dig for vinyl in his style. Deep underground hip-hop, jazz, soul, lo-fi. 🎵 A Note on Nujabes

Jun Seba (瀬葉純) was born 1974 in Tokyo, died February 2010 in a traffic accident. His label was Hydeout Productions, based out of his Shibuya record shops.
He composed the entire soundtrack for Samurai Champloo (2004) — if you haven't watched it before this trip, do. It's set in Edo-period Japan with hip-hop sensibility and will reframe how you see Kyoto and Nara.
His two albums — Metaphorical Music (2003) and Modal Soul (2005) — are worth picking up on vinyl at Face Records. Originals are rare and expensive; reissues are available.
The word "nujabes" is his real name — Jun Seba — spelled backwards. A quiet, characteristic move from someone who preferred to stay in the shadows of his own music.

Top Photo Spots
& When to Go

September–October is Japan's most photogenic season. Autumn colour, morning mist, raking golden light. These are the shots that define the trip.

01
Fushimi Inari at Dawn
📍 Kyoto
5am. Tunnels of vermilion torii gates winding up a forest mountain. Zero tourists. Stone lanterns still lit. Foxes occasionally visible on the mountain path. The most recognizable image in Japan — best experienced at the hour when it's completely yours.

Miyajima Torii at Sunrise
📍 Hiroshima Bay
Stay overnight on the island. At low tide walk across the mudflat to the O-torii gate as the first light turns it deep vermilion. The gate reflected in tide pools, Mt. Misen behind, zero crowds. One of Japan's three great iconic images.
💡 Check tide tables. Low tide 6–7am is ideal.
03
Chureito Pagoda + Mt. Fuji
📍 Fujiyoshida
The five-storey pagoda on a hillside with Mt. Fuji as backdrop — the most-reproduced image of Japan in existence. 398 stone steps to reach it. Go at sunrise before mist clears. In late October, the maple trees around the pagoda begin turning.
💡 Best clear mornings mid-September. Check Fuji webcam before going.
04
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
📍 Kyoto
30-metre bamboo walls forming a green cathedral over a narrow path. The light filtering through at 6am, the clicking of bamboo in the wind, and the complete absence of people make this one of Japan's most extraordinary sensory experiences.
💡 5:45am arrival. Bring a tripod for the diffused tunnel shot.
05
Shibuya Sky at Sunset
📍 Shibuya, Tokyo
The rooftop observation deck of Scramble Square, 230m up, gives the fullest view of the crossing below. Sunset in September turns the whole city copper. Book the specific 17:30 slot in advance — timed entry system.
💡 Book timed entry online. Sep golden hour ~17:45.
06
Sannenzaka / Ninenzaka Lanes
📍 Higashiyama, Kyoto
Stone-cobble alleyways flanked by Edo-period wooden teahouses, lanterns, and bamboo fences. In October, small maples in the courtyards start turning red. Best at dusk when paper lanterns glow and the stone is damp from evening rain.
💡 7pm on a weeknight. Rain improves the shot significantly.
07
Daisetsuzan Asahidake Peak
📍 Hokkaido
At 1,600m+ in mid-October: volcanic steam vents, alpine tundra in full crimson/gold/orange, distant volcanic summits. The widest and most dramatic autumn colour panorama in Japan. Absolutely no crowds beyond the ropeway top station.
💡 7am ropeway. Bring a 24–70mm. The volcanic steam adds dramatic atmosphere.
08
Ōkunoin Cemetery at Dusk
📍 Kōya-san
Two kilometres of 200,000 stone lanterns and ancient moss-covered tombs under enormous cedar trees. As dusk falls and lanterns begin to glow through the forest, the atmosphere is unlike anything else in Japan. Silent, sacred, spectacular.
💡 Start the path at 5pm — arrive at lantern glow time. Tripod essential.
09
Lake Ashi Fuji Reflection
📍 Hakone
On windless clear September mornings, Mt. Fuji reflects perfectly in Lake Ashi. The classic spot is on the northwest shore near Moto-Hakone. 6am from the lakeside path. The reflection sometimes appears before Fuji is fully visible through morning mist.
💡 Windless mornings only. Check weather 2 days ahead.

Ginzan Onsen at Night
📍 Yamagata, Tohoku
Taisho-era wooden ryokan on both sides of a narrow river, each window glowing amber, hot spring steam rising into cool October air. The single most photogenic onsen town in Japan. Almost no foreign tourists. A shot that looks like concept art from a Miyazaki film.

Logistics · Money · Transport · Timing

Essential Tips
& Logistics

Practical knowledge that saves time, money and frustration across 45 days, including the book-ahead ticket calendar.

🚅 Transport

Do a JR Pass vs individual tickets check once the route is final. The current budget assumes either a 21/14-day pass mix or individual Shinkansen tickets depending on final routing.
IC Card (Suica or Pasmo) is essential — load ¥5,000–10,000 and tap on/off every metro, bus, and convenience store in Japan.
Shinkansen is the default for anything over 90 minutes. Overnight buses are cheap alternatives (¥3,000–6,000) for Osaka-Tokyo if you want to save a hotel night.
Taxis are expensive (~¥700 flag-fall). Use for short late-night journeys only. Uber works in major cities; Go app works everywhere.
For Hokkaido, rent a car in Sapporo for the Daisetsuzan + Lake Tōya + Noboribetsu loop — public transport to these is poor and a car takes you to the best spots.
Domestic flights: Tokyo–Sapporo is 1.5 hours by ANA/JAL. Book 2–3 months ahead for ¥6,000–12,000. Better than the 10h Shinkansen for Hokkaido.

💴 Money & Budget

Japan is still largely cash-heavy, especially in rural areas, temples and small restaurants. Always carry ¥10,000–20,000 in cash.
7-Eleven ATMs accept all international cards and dispense cash 24/7. This is your most reliable ATM source across all 45 days.
Mid-range daily budget (excluding big experiences and fine dining): ¥15,000–25,000/day covers food, transport, entry fees and accommodation.
Tipping is not just unnecessary — it can cause offense. Never tip anyone anywhere. The service is world-class and the pride in service is cultural.
The Ichiran ramen model (solo booth dining) is perfect if you're eating alone — order on a form, no interaction needed, ¥1,000 including extras.
100-yen shops (Daiso, Seria) are extraordinary — buy chopsticks, small containers, phone accessories, umbrellas. Don't underestimate them.

🍁 Autumn Foliage Timing (Sep–Oct)

Mid-September: Hokkaido Daisetsuzan alpine zone peaks first. Visit early in trip if Hokkaido is planned at start.
Late September: Hokkaido lowlands, Naruko Onsen Gorge (Tohoku), Nikko forests. Plan days 11–18 here.
Early October: Jigokudani Monkey Park, Nagano Alps, Karuizawa. Days 17–18 hit this perfectly.
Mid October: Kyoto begins — Philosopher's Path, Arashiyama, Tōfuku-ji illuminations start. Days 19–28 timing is ideal.
Late October: Tokyo — Shinjuku Gyoen, Ueno Park begin turning. Your final Hokkaido phase catches peak Daisetsuzan colour again.
Check koyo.walkerplus.com (Japan autumn forecast site) 2 weeks ahead for exact timing adjustments.
